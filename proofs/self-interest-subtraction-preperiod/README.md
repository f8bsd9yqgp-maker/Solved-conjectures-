# Explicit preperiod bound for self-interest cumulative subtraction games

AI-assisted research note, 25 November 2026 (US Central). A proposed solution to Problem 3 of A. Bhagat, T. Kulkarni, U. Larsson and D. Murali, *Tie-breaking in self interest cumulative subtraction games*, arXiv:2510.24280v2 (20 January 2026), Section 6: https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24280v2#S6 .

## Theorem
Let S be a nonempty finite subset of the positive integers, M=max S, and consider the four fixed friendly/antagonistic convention profiles of Definition 2. Their pairwise coordinate discrepancies are eventually periodic with a common period P, and both P and a common preperiod threshold T are bounded by 2M[1+C^(4M)], where C=M(M+1)/2. This does not assert P=2M or settle Problem 2.

## Proof
Let U_c(h)=(a,b) be the mover and other player's optimal future scores for convention profile c. Write h=qM+r, 0<=r<M, and set L(h)=(M ceil(q/2), M floor(q/2)). The mover can secure the first coordinate of L by playing M on each of the first ceil(q/2) own turns: before the j-th own turn at most (2j-2)M pebbles have been removed. The other player similarly secures the second coordinate by playing M on each of the first floor(q/2) own turns; before their j-th own turn at most (2j-1)M pebbles have been removed. These turns necessarily exist whenever the indicated inequality leaves at least M pebbles. Both guarantees apply to the subgame-perfect equilibrium, since tie-breaking only distinguishes moves maximizing own utility. The total utility is at most h. Hence V_c(h)=U_c(h)-L(h) is an integer pair (x,y) with x,y>=0 and x+y<=r. At most C distinct pairs occur, since (r+1)(r+2)/2<=C.

For each c the recurrence picks from (s+b_d(h-s),a_d(h-s)) for legal s in S, where d is the swapped convention profile. It maximizes the first coordinate, then maximizes or minimizes the second coordinate according to the mover's friendly or antagonistic identity. Equal pairs need no further tie-break. At h>=M every s is legal. The recurrence only consults the preceding M positions across the four profiles. Moreover L(h+2M)=L(h)+(M,M). Translating every continuation outcome by (M,M) translates every candidate pair and the selected pair by the same vector. Thus the recurrence on V has a deterministic transition depending only on h modulo 2M and the previous M normalized outcome rows.

Sample the normalized window W_k=(V_c(j): c in the four profiles, 2Mk-M+1<=j<=2Mk) for k>=1. Each has at most C^(4M) possible values; the sample phase is fixed and its transition to W_(k+1) is deterministic. Among the first C^(4M)+1 sampled windows two coincide, say W_i=W_j, i<j. Induction by the normalized recurrence gives V_c(h+2M(j-i))=V_c(h) for every h>=2Mi-M+1 and every c. Since L is shared across conventions, their discrepancies equal differences of V and inherit this periodicity. Taking T=2Mi and P=2M(j-i) proves the stated bound. QED.

## Scope and priority
This supplies an explicit (not optimized) universal bound on the preperiod for the actual eventual period, as asked in Problem 3. If Problem 3 is interpreted as requiring specifically the conjectural period 2M, that stronger requirement remains unresolved. The argument also gives coordinate discrepancy <= h mod M <= M-1. Conjecture 17 itself is not claimed as newly solved: a prior proof by Shivam Patel is posted at https://mathdb.com/p/371185/bounded-discrepancies-between-tie-breaking-conventions with a different, weaker bound. Focused literature searches and inspection of both repository README indexes on 25 November 2026 found no earlier proof of this explicit preperiod result. This is not exhaustive priority verification, and independent mathematical review is pending.
