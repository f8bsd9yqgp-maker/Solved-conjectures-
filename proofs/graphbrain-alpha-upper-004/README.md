## Statement
α≤ceil(exp(girth))+M.

## Disproof
For odd r≥51, take r disjoint copies of K5, designate a hub in each, and add bridges joining consecutive hubs into a path. Write G_r for this graph. Its independence number is r: each clique contributes at most one vertex, and choosing one nonhub per clique attains the bound. Its maximum degree is six and minimum degree four. Girth is three. There are exactly 10r triangles, all confined to cliques. The maximum common-neighbor count is three: same-clique pairs have three common neighbors, and distinct-clique pairs have at most one. The middle hub is the unique center: hub j has eccentricity 1+max(j−1,r−j), and each nonhub has eccentricity one greater.

Write M for max_even_minus_even_horizontal under the author's definition. Fix any root vertex in clique j. The even-distance vertices of that clique consist of the root alone. Every other clique contributes either its hub alone, or all four nonhubs (which induce K4). These types alternate along each side of j because distances to hubs of consecutive cliques differ by one, and nonhub distances exceed the corresponding hub distance by one. Let H,Q count the hub-only and four-nonhub cliques. Then H+Q=r−1 and H−Q≤2 (at most one excess hub-only clique on each side). The contribution to |Even|−|E(G[Even])| is at most 1+H−2Q≤3−Q, since each K4 contributes 4−6=−2 and ignoring cross-clique edges only increases this expression. Since Q≥(r−3)/2, it is negative. Thus the clamped maximum M=0 exactly. 

For later use, distance between vertices in cliques i,j is at most |i−j|+2, even if they lie in the same clique. Averaging over distinct ordered vertex pairs yields average distance at most 2+5(r²−1)/(3(5r−1))<2+r/2<r for r≥51.
Since e<3 (from the exponential series), ceil(e³)≤27. The proposed bound is at most 27<r. ∎
