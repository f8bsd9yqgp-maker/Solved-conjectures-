# Diversified-tolerance majority list edge-colouring: candidate resolution of Conjecture 23

27 November 2026. AI-assisted analytical argument; independent review, formal verification and priority confirmation pending.

## Statement and source
P. Pękała and J. Przybyło, On List Extensions of the Majority Edge Colourings, Electronic Journal of Combinatorics 32(4) (2025), DOI 10.37236/13882, arXiv:2502.12688v1, Section 7, Conjecture 23. https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12688v1 . The conjecture asks for a threshold O(ell/epsilon) for finite graphs, where each edge list has size at most ell, every color c has tolerance alpha(c) in (0,1), and the sum of tolerances in each list is at least 1+epsilon. A valid coloring selects one listed color per edge and has d_c(v) <= alpha(c)d(v) for all vertices and colors. The paper's general bounded-list sufficient threshold is O(ell^2 epsilon^-2).

## Lemma (one-sided additive-two rounding)
Let G be a finite loopless graph, with finite nonempty edge lists. Suppose x_(e,c) >= 0 for c in L(e), with sum_c x_(e,c)=1 for each edge e. Set load F_(v,c)=sum_{e incident to v} x_(e,c). There is a list coloring with d_c(v) <= F_(v,c)+2 for every v,c; moreover any variable initially zero stays zero.

Proof. Fix variables equal to 0 or 1 permanently, retain the edge-sum equations, and initially retain every vertex-color load equation. At a generic stage let N count the strictly fractional variables, M count edges with fractional variables, and R count retained vertex-color equations containing fractional variables. Every such edge has at least two fractional variables, and their sum equals 1, since no such edge can have a fixed 1. Hence N>=2M. For a retained vertex-color row r define its upward potential U_r=sum(1-x_i) over its fractional variables. Each variable belongs to at most two retained vertex-color equations (one per endpoint). Therefore sum_r U_r <= 2 sum_i(1-x_i)=2(N-M).

If any retained row containing fractional variables has U_r<=2, drop that row permanently. Its load at the moment of dropping equals its initial load (since it was retained until that moment). Future increases can only come from variables fractional at dropping; each can increase by at most 1-x_i. Hence its final load is at most its original load plus 2. Rows containing no fractional variables can simply be retained or ignored, as all their variables are permanently fixed.

Otherwise, if R>0, every retained row has U_r>2, giving R<N-M. If R=0 and N>0, also M<N. In either case there are strictly fewer than N equations on the fractional variables: M+R<N. A nonzero real null direction exists for their homogeneous system. Move in that direction by the largest positive distance that keeps every fractional variable in [0,1]. This distance is positive and finite, and at least one additional variable reaches 0 or 1. All retained sums stay unchanged. Fix all newly integral variables permanently. Repeat. Each step drops a row or fixes a variable; both finite, so eventually all variables are integral. The edge equations ensure exactly one color per edge. Dropped rows have the bound proved above, and never-dropped rows have exact original load. QED.

## Theorem (Conjecture 23)
Assume the hypotheses above and delta(G)>=ceil(2ell/epsilon). Put eta=epsilon/ell, and beta_c=max(0,alpha(c)-eta). For every edge e, B_e=sum_{c in L(e)} beta_c >= sum alpha(c)-eta |L(e)| >=1. Set x_(e,c)=beta_c/B_e. This is a fractional assignment supported on listed colors and x_(e,c)<=beta_c. If beta_c=0, the rounding lemma never uses color c on any edge. If beta_c>0, for every vertex v the fractional load is F_(v,c)<=d(v)(alpha(c)-eta). The rounding lemma gives d_c(v)<=F_(v,c)+2<=alpha(c)d(v), since eta d(v)>=2. Thus the asserted coloring exists. The explicit threshold 2ell/epsilon (or its ceiling) has the requested asymptotic order. For nonempty lists satisfying the assumptions epsilon<ell-1, so rounding up does not change the order even if parameters vary jointly. QED.

## Corollary (Conjecture 25)
For a common palette C of at most ell colors and sum alpha(c)>=1+epsilon, assign C as the list of every edge and apply the theorem. This establishes the corresponding non-list Conjecture 25 with the same threshold. This is a logical corollary, not a separate technique.

## Related extension and parameter caveat
With a positive tolerance lower bound a and no bound on list length, use x_(e,c)=alpha(c)/sum_{b in L(e)}alpha(b). Its load is at most d(v)alpha(c)/(1+epsilon). The lemma yields a sufficient minimum degree ceil(2(1+epsilon)/(a epsilon)). This gives the requested scaling in Conjectures 24 and 26 for 0<epsilon<=1. For unbounded epsilon the uniform O(1/(a epsilon)) expression cannot hold literally over all parameters: set all tolerances to a, take enough colors to satisfy the excess, and take a nonempty regular graph of degree d<1/a; no color can occur even once at a vertex. The sufficient general expression is O((1+epsilon)/(a epsilon)); no unconditional claim about the authors' intended asymptotic convention is made here.

## Transfer of methods
Achour: isolate local capacity constraints and track the effect of local changes. Eulerian strong-majority: use a local null-direction exchange, preserve invariants and force progress to a boundary. Ordinary majority: introduce explicit slack before eliminating fractional degrees of freedom. List-majority: retain each edge's permitted colors and count its two endpoint interfaces; replace equal-capacity bipartite detachment by one-sided sparse rounding. No earlier candidate proof is a logical dependency. The rounding method is a standard family of discrepancy/iterative-relaxation techniques; novelty of the lemma itself is not asserted.
