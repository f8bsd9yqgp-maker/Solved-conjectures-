# Graffiti.pc Conjecture 393a — 2-domination and matching in the second pendant neighborhood

24 November 2026 (US Central). AI-assisted written mathematical note; independent review and Lean verification remain pending. The statement is from the [August 2026 transcription of Written on the Wall II](https://github.com/Kuberwastaken/c5-k4/blob/main/corpora/graffiti_pc_wow2.json), which lists the entry open. The November 2026 PDF is indexed at https://www.uhd.edu/academics/sciences/mathematics-statistics/wowii/files/wowiiaii.pdf, but its later pages could not be retrieved and screenshot requests failed; latest official status is therefore unconfirmed. Throughout let G be a finite simple connected graph on n>2 vertices, P the pendant (degree-one) vertices, S=N(P) their support vertices, X=N(S), with N the union of open neighborhoods, and γ₂ the minimum size of a set D such that every vertex outside D has at least two neighbors in D. P and S are disjoint, P⊆X, and each pendant has exactly one support neighbor.

The conjecture is γ₂(G)≤n−|X|+μ(G[X])+|P|. Put Y=X∖(S∪P) and D₀=(V∖X)∪S∪P. A vertex outside D₀ belongs to Y and has a neighbor in S. If it has fewer than two D₀-neighbors, it has a neighbor in Y because its degree is at least two (it is not a pendant). Thus adjoining a dominating set of the nonisolated part of G[Y] yields a 2-dominating set. A graph without isolated vertices has domination number at most its matching number: take a maximum matching, and choose one endpoint per matched edge, choosing an endpoint adjacent to unmatched vertices if possible. If both endpoints have unmatched neighbors, those neighbors must be the same single vertex, or a length-three augmenting path exists. Unmatched vertices have a matched neighbor; every matched edge is dominated by the selected endpoint. The resulting set dominates. Hence γ₂≤|D₀|+μ(G[Y])=n−|X|+|P|+|S∩X|+μ(G[Y]). Each support in S∩X can be matched to a distinct private leaf in P⊆X, disjoint from a maximum matching of G[Y]. Therefore μ(G[X])≥|S∩X|+μ(G[Y]), proving the conjecture. ∎

This also follows by specializing the neighborhood 2-domination theorem in DeLaViña, Larson, Pepper and Waller, [Graffiti.pc on the 2-domination number of a graph](https://math1um.github.io/Research/r07.pdf), Theorem 23. This is a corollary of established techniques; no novelty is asserted. No exact earlier numbered resolution was identified in targeted searches.
