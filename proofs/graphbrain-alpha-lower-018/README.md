## Original statement
`independence_number(x) >= minimum(inverse_degree(x), floor(lovasz_theta(x)))`

## Counterexample
Let H be the triple lexicographic power of C5. Its independence number is 8: independence numbers multiply under lexicographic products, by projection to the outer graph and counting each fiber. Its Lovasz theta number is sqrt(125), using the classical value theta(C5)=sqrt(5) and multiplicativity under lexicographic products (see https://doi.org/10.3390/e28090994). Attach twenty disjoint two-edge pendant paths at a vertex of H. Alpha is at most 28 by partitioning into H and twenty two-vertex edges. The inverse-degree sum is at least 20*(1+1/2)=30 from the arms alone. The induced subgraph consisting of H and twenty terminal leaves is the disjoint union of H and twenty isolated vertices. By monotonicity on induced subgraphs and additivity on disjoint unions, theta of the whole graph is at least sqrt(125)+20>31. The claimed minimum is therefore at least 30>28. The counterexample uses established theta properties, not a new theorem on theta. ∎
