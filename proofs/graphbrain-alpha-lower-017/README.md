## Statement
α≥n_-/2−c.

## Disproof
Let B(r,k) consist of r disjoint K_k cliques, each with one distinguished hub, and join consecutive hubs by one bridge. Let A(r,k) be B(r,k) with an extra two-edge pendant path at each end hub. Then α(B)=r and α(A)=r+2: partition into the r cliques and (when present) two additional edges, and choose one nonhub in each clique and the two terminal leaves. For even k, perfect matchings within each clique and the additional edges give μ(B)=rk/2 and μ(A)=rk/2+2. For r≥3 the two terminal leaves of A are exactly its periphery, since their distance r+3 exceeds every other pair distance. Girth is three for k≥3.
Use B(r,8) for r≥4. In each clique the seven nonhub vertices support a six-dimensional subspace of vectors with sum zero; extend by zero elsewhere. The adjacency matrix acts as minus the identity on this subspace, because the only external edges touch the hub and the sum at the hub is zero. Thus there are at least 6r negative eigenvalues counted with multiplicity. The maximum number c of common neighbors is six: within a clique it is six, and between different cliques it is at most one. Hence the claimed lower bound is at least 3r−6>r=α. At r=4, numerical symmetric eigensolver gives n_-=27 and the claimed bound 15/2; the exact subspace argument already gives at least 6>4. ∎
