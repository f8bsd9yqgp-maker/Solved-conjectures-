# Complete bipartite prism q-zero-forcing conjecture: proof

AI-assisted mathematical note, 25 November 2026. Independent academic review and discovery priority remain unverified.

Source: S. Fallat, N. Joshi, R. Maleki, K. Meagher, S. A. Mojallal, S. Nasserasr, M. N. Shirazi, A. S. Razafimahatratra and B. Stevens, *The q-Analogue of Zero Forcing for Certain Families of Graphs*, arXiv:2306.01138 (2023), Conjecture 4.4, https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01138; journal version, Discrete Applied Mathematics 348 (2024), 91–104, DOI 10.1016/j.dam.2024.01.014. The preprint establishes bounds and discusses the q=0 case. The principal contribution here is the missing q>=1 lower bound.

## Theorem

For integers n,m>=2, let G=K_(n,m) square K_2. Then Z_0(G)=2 min(n,m), and Z_q(G)=n+m for every integer q>=1.

Use the standard q-forcing rules: a token colors any white vertex, ordinary forcing colors the unique white neighbor of a blue vertex, and an oracle move offers at least q+1 connected components of the white induced graph. The oracle returns a nonempty subcollection; ordinary forcing is allowed in the induced graph on all blue vertices and the returned white vertices. The player must succeed against every oracle response. Monotonicity gives Z_0<=Z_1<=...<=Z, where Z is ordinary zero forcing.

The two layers of G are copies of K_(n,m). A rung is the vertical matching edge joining the two copies of a vertex. There are n rungs of part A and m of part B. An untouched rung has two white endpoints; its first touch means its first endpoint is colored. A full rung has both endpoints blue. A single rung has exactly one blue endpoint.

## Lower bound for q=1

**Oracle strategy.** If an offered white component contains no untouched rung, return that component alone. Otherwise all offered components contain untouched rungs. Choose two. If untouched rungs existed in both parts, every white vertex would be connected to a white endpoint of an opposite-part untouched rung, and those opposite rungs would be connected to each other. All white vertices would then lie in a single component. Hence the two chosen components contain untouched rungs of the same part. If any white vertex of the opposite part existed, it would connect those untouched rungs in its layer, a contradiction. Therefore all white vertices lie in one part, and the chosen components are individual untouched rungs. Return both. No force can occur in the resulting induced graph: each blue vertex of the opposite part has two returned white neighbors in its layer, and a blue vertex of the same part has no returned white neighbors. Thus no oracle move first touches a rung. This remains true if several consecutive free forces are permitted within a returned subgraph.

Every free first touch must consequently be ordinary. It cannot be vertical (the vertical partner is white). A horizontal force has a blue source in the opposite part and a unique white target in the relevant layer. In particular no other untouched rung of the target part exists. There can thus be at most one free first touch per part, at most two total.

Let F be the total number of free first touches in a successful play against this oracle, and D the number of tokens used to complete previously touched rungs. Every paid first touch uses one token. The total token count is n+m-F+D. If it is less than n+m, then F>D, so either D=0 and F>=1, or D=1 and F=2.

**Observation while both parts have untouched rungs.** All white vertices are connected, so no oracle move is possible. A vertical ordinary force is impossible since its source has a white opposite-part neighbor in its layer. A horizontal force must originate on a full rung (otherwise the white vertical neighbor would be an additional white neighbor). Its target must be on the last untouched rung of its part: the untouched rung supplies a white neighbor in that layer, so the unique white target must be that endpoint. Consequently no free move other than an ordinary first touch from a full rung is possible.

**Trap lemma.** Suppose every A rung is single with its blue endpoint in the same layer l, and B still has an untouched rung. As long as no further completion token is spent, A retains this configuration and no free first touch of B can occur. Indeed, a vertical force to an A vertex is blocked by the untouched B rung in the source layer. A horizontal force to a white A vertex is blocked by the n>=2 white A vertices in its layer. A horizontal first touch of B is impossible from A since every blue A vertex still has its white vertical partner. All white A vertices and all untouched B rungs are in one white component (the white A layer is adjacent to the B endpoints there). Under the oracle strategy that component cannot be returned: if offered, every other offered component contains no untouched rung, and one of those is returned instead. If not offered, it cannot be returned either. Thus no oracle force changes A or first touches B. No paid first touch changes A because A has no untouched rung. Induction preserves the trap until B has no untouched rung; in particular B cannot acquire a free first touch.

**Case D=0.** Until the first part runs out of untouched rungs, there is no full rung and no free move: initially none is full, paid first touches do not make full rungs, and the observation rules out a first free move. So the first exhaustion is by tokens, say in part A, with all A rungs single and all touched B rungs single. If the A blue endpoints occupy both layers, each layer contains white A vertices. All white vertices remain connected as long as B has an untouched rung: each white B vertex links to a white A vertex in its layer, and an untouched B rung connects the two layers of white A vertices. Vertical forces in A are blocked by the untouched B rung, vertical forces in B are blocked by white A vertices, and horizontal forces require a full rung. There are no full rungs and no completion tokens; consequently only paid first touches of B are possible until B is exhausted. If instead A is uniformly blue in one layer, the trap lemma gives the same conclusion about free first touches. In either case F=0, a contradiction.

**Case D=1 and F=2.** The first free first touch must occur while both parts still have untouched rungs, because only a part with an untouched rung can receive a first touch, and a part cannot receive two free first touches. Before this force no free moves occurred by the observation. The source is a full rung, so the only completion token has already been used there. Say the source is in B and the target in A in layer l. No A rung is full, and the target is the sole white A vertex in layer l; hence all other A rungs are single and blue in layer l. After the force every A rung is single and blue in layer l. B still has an untouched rung and there are no more completion tokens. The trap lemma prevents a free first touch in B, contradicting F=2.

Both possibilities are impossible. Therefore Z_1>=n+m. Coloring an entire layer with n+m tokens permits each blue vertex to force its vertical partner by ordinary forcing, so Z<=n+m. Monotonicity proves the q>=1 formula.

## Independent q=0 argument

Assume n<=m by symmetry. Color every A vertex (2n tokens). The remaining white components are individual B rungs. Offer one such component, which the oracle must return. A blue A vertex in either layer forces the B vertex in that layer, and its partner can then be forced vertically. Repeat. Thus Z_0<=2n.

For the lower bound construct a positive semidefinite Gram matrix of rank 2m with the off-diagonal zero pattern of G. Let e_i,f_i (1<=i<=m) be an orthonormal basis of R^(2m). Let u_1,...,u_n be orthogonal rows of a dense orthogonal-up-to-scaling m-by-m matrix: for m>=3 use mI-2J, where J is all ones; for m=2 use [[3,4],[4,-3]]. All entries are nonzero and rows are mutually orthogonal.

Assign B-layer-0 vertex i vector e_i, and B-layer-1 vertex i vector e_i+f_i. Assign A-layer-0 vertex j vector sum_i u_ji(e_i-f_i), and A-layer-1 vertex j vector sum_i u_ji f_i. Their inner products are nonzero exactly on graph edges among distinct vertices: the same-layer A-B products are u_ji, crossed-layer A-B products vanish, same-part same-layer off-diagonal products vanish, and same-part crossed-layer products are nonzero exactly along matching rungs. The B vectors span the ambient space, so the Gram matrix has rank 2m and nullity 2(n+m)-2m=2n.

By the positive semidefinite maximum-nullity lower bound for Z_0 (Theorem 1.1 in the source), Z_0>=2n. This completes the proof.

## Reproduction and limitations

Run python3 verify.py (standard library only). It checks the Gram sparsity and rank construction for nine parameter pairs, and exhaustively computes the optimal token count in a relaxation of play against the proof oracle. The relaxation permits every paid move, every ordinary force, and every force within any white component containing no untouched rung, even if an actual oracle offer would be illegal. The other possible oracle response (two components with untouched rungs) is checked to permit no force. Repeated restricted forcing is also covered: a component may split after a force, and any further force in a union of its pieces is permitted in the component containing its target. The computed relaxed minimum equals n+m in all nine pairs; 238,848 white configurations were visited. This is a finite sanity check of the lower-bound strategy, not a substitute for the unrestricted proof.

The preprint, journal bibliographic record and subsequent literature were searched; no earlier resolution of the missing prism bound was located in the limited search. This does not establish priority. No external peer review or proof-assistant formalization was performed. The q=0 case is included for completeness, not claimed as a novel result.
