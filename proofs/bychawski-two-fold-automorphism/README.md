# Bychawski's random graph two-fold automorphism edit conjecture

**Status:** AI-assisted written proof candidate, November 24–25, 2026; independent review, priority and Lean verification pending.

Source: Bartłomiej Bychawski, [Constructions of graphs with any possible two-fold automorphism and automorphism groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06267), Conjecture 6.5. This is not the related expectation conjecture 6.6.

## Statement and stronger claim

For a uniform labeled random graph G on n vertices and fixed ε>0, the probability that at most n(1-ε)/2 edge additions/deletions can create a nontrivial two-fold automorphism projection tends to zero.

Let D(G) be the smallest number of edits achieving such a projection, and d(u,v) the number of external vertices w for which A_uw≠A_vw. With probability tending to one,
$$D(G)=\min_{u\ne v}d(u,v).$$
If the target is required to be reduced (all open neighborhoods distinct), the corresponding minimum is
$$D_{\rm red}(G)=\min_{u\ne v}\{d(u,v)+1-A_{uv}\}.$$

## Proof

A two-fold automorphism is a permutation pair (p,q) satisfying A_ij=A_{p(i),q(j)} for all ordered pairs i,j. Nontrivial first-coordinate projection means p is nonidentity; allowing either coordinate nonidentity gives the same minimum by symmetry. Put S=supp(p)∪supp(q), k=|S|. A vertex outside S is fixed by both permutations, and its adjacency bits on S must be constant on each orbit of the subgroup generated by p,q.

For 3≤k≤63, if k=3 there is a single orbit of size three, and for each outside vertex the three bits require at least one edit unless all agree. Hence the necessary edit count dominates Bin(n-3,3/4). For 4≤k≤63 there are two disjoint pairs lying within orbits (every orbit on S has size at least two). Each pair per outside vertex gives an independent disagreement, so the count dominates Bin(2(n-k),1/2). In either case Hoeffding's inequality gives an exponentially small probability of at most 2n/3 edits for a fixed pair of permutations. For fixed k the number of pairs is at most n^(2k) (choose S, then two permutations on S; more precisely binom(n,k)(k!)²≤n^(2k)). A union bound over this finite range tends to zero.

For k≥64, interchange p and q if necessary so p moves at least k/2 vertices. For each moved i, among j there are at most three exceptions to the constraint equating two distinct off-diagonal unordered adjacency variables: j=i, q(j)=p(i), or j=p(i) with q(j)=i. Thus at least k(n-3)/2 ordered constraints equate distinct independent random edge variables. Form a loopless constraint multigraph on unordered edge variables. Each variable appears at most twice as source and twice as target, so degree is at most four including multiplicities. Greedily selecting a matching removes at most seven constraints per selected edge (parallel edges only improve this bound). In particular a matching has M≥k(n-3)/16 edges. The matching is chosen from the permutations alone, not from the random graph, and its disagreements are independent Bernoulli(1/2). Each disagreement forces a distinct edit.

For n≥6 we have M≥kn/32≥2n. By Hoeffding,
$$\Pr[\operatorname{Bin}(M,1/2)\le 2n/3]\le \exp(-M/18)\le\exp(-kn/576).$$
Indeed 2n/3≤M/3. Summing n^(2k) times this bound over 64≤k≤n tends to zero. Together the two ranges show that with high probability all permutation pairs of combined support at least three require more than 2n/3 edits.

The only remaining nontrivial pairs have support exactly two, with p,q each either identity or the same transposition of u,v. All require the external neighborhoods to coincide, costing at least d(u,v). If exactly one permutation is a transposition, the diagonal constraints require A_uv=0; if both are transpositions A_uv is unrestricted. The ordinary automorphism (p,p) is therefore achieved with exactly d(u,v) edits. A reduced target requires A_uv=1, giving the extra cost 1-A_uv.

To verify the reduced construction, with high probability every original pair has at least three external neighborhood differences, by a binomial tail and union bound. Copy all external bits of u to v and set uv=1. For any pair of vertices other than u,v, not involving v, only the witness coordinate v can have changed, so distinct neighborhoods remain. For v and a third vertex z, the original u,z neighborhood difference outside u,v,z has at least two witnesses; their corresponding bits in the new v,z neighborhoods still differ. The pair u,v is distinguished by the edge uv. Thus the target is reduced. For a fixed pair d(u,v) is Bin(n-2,1/2), so even with one additional edit its cost is at most 2n/3 with high probability. This proves both exact formulas.

Finally a Chernoff bound and union bound over pairs show that the probability that any d(u,v)≤n(1-ε)/2 is o(1) for 0<ε<1. The fixed-pair upper bound similarly gives D(G)/n→1/2 in probability. For ε=1 the lower-tail conclusion follows from any smaller positive ε; for ε>1 the event is empty. This establishes Conjecture 6.5 under either target convention.

## Limitations

This is an analytic candidate, not a formalized or independently refereed proof. Searches in both repositories for arXiv:2406.06267, Bychawski and two-fold automorphisms found no matching entry at the time of research. This does not establish worldwide priority. Ordinary graph automorphisms are included in the two-fold projection group; the statement does not assert the same result for creating a strictly non-diagonal two-fold automorphism in a twin-free graph.
