## Statement
alpha ≥ floor(atanh(sin(triangle_count))).

## Disproof
For rigorous trigonometric comparisons use radians and rational bounds L=3.14159265358979<pi<U=3.14159265358980. They follow from Machin's identity pi=16 arctan(1/5)−4 arctan(1/239), with alternating series bounds using 14 and 5 terms, respectively, as checked in the verifier. For 0<epsilon<1, sin(epsilon)≤epsilon and cos(epsilon)≥1−epsilon²/2. If x=(odd integer)pi/2−epsilon, then tan(x)=cot(epsilon)≥(1−epsilon²/2)/epsilon. Use the upper rational bound on epsilon on the right, a decreasing function. The verifier computes exact rational margins.
Take K11, with alpha=1 and triangle count 165. The angle is within 1/10 of 105pi/2, where sine is one. Hence sin(165)>1−(1/10)^2/2=199/200. This exceeds tanh(2)=(e^4−1)/(e^4+1), since e<11/4. Also sin(165)<1 (pi irrational). Thus atanh(sin(165))>2 and RHS≥2>1.
